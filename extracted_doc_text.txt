A2. Purpose of the Foundation
Over the past year, the Foundation listened to the stories of aspiring students, determined women and bright young children. Each had a dream but little access to opportunity. Many wanted to enter the IT field, to learn, to grow and to contribute, but poverty, lack of resources and social barriers stood in the way.
Addithalam Foundation was born from those stories. The founders saw talent, resilience and untapped potential, and decided to act. They began offering free, high-quality IT training and learning programs "to turn dreams into careers and struggles into success stories."
About-page addition: Through skill-building, mentorship and compassionate support, the Foundation strives to empower the underserved and create a future where no dream is too distant and no voice goes unheard.

Part B: International-Grade UI/UX Principles
These principles come from 2026 research on nonprofit website design, WCAG 2.2 accessibility, Core Web Vitals, mobile UX and Indian NGO trust requirements. Each principle lists what the research found, then what it means in practice. Source names are given in brackets and full links appear in Part D.
How to read this part: "Evidence" summarises published findings. "In practice" is a design instruction. Where a statistic comes from a vendor or agency blog, treat it as directional rather than guaranteed.
B1. Clarity above the fold (the five-second test)
Evidence
A homepage should tell a visitor who you are, what you do, why it matters and how to help within about five seconds, starting with a clear mission headline [Bloomerang, 2026].
Clarity beats polish. A useful test is whether the site could belong to any other nonprofit in the sector [Elevation Web, 2026 checklist].
Girls Who Code puts its mission statement at the centre of the hero, backed by imagery of the girls it serves [Kanopi].
In practice
Hero headline states the outcome ("Free IT training that turns talent into careers") rather than a generic slogan.
One supporting sentence naming who it is for (students, women, young learners) and that it is free.
Two buttons only: a primary action (Join a Program) and a secondary action (Donate or Volunteer).
Use a real photograph of learners in a Foundation class instead of a stock image.
B2. Pathways for every audience
Evidence
Nonprofit sites should serve donors, funders, decision-makers and activists; sites built like brochures underperform [DesignRush, 2026].
Volunteer pathway design matters as much as donation pathway design for organisations that rely on volunteer labour [KrishaWeb].
In practice
Add a three-door section on the homepage: "I want to learn", "I want to volunteer or partner", "I want to support".
Give each door its own landing page with a single clear action and an outcome statement.
Add a dedicated CSR and partner page for companies, since Indian corporates fund much IT education.
B3. Mobile-first design
Evidence
More than 60 per cent of nonprofit traffic comes from smartphones [Bloomerang, 2026]; about 60 per cent of global web traffic is mobile [Incremys, 2026].
Among visitors who reached a primary donation page, 11 per cent on desktop completed a gift versus 8 per cent on mobile [M+R Benchmarks 2026, via DesignRush]. Only 1.6 per cent of nonprofit visitors donated in 2025 [same source].
The 2026 benchmark for a donation form is completion in under two minutes on a phone with no friction [Big Sea, 2026].
In practice
Design every screen at 360 px width first, then enhance for tablet and desktop.
Treat Join, Donate and Volunteer forms as the most important screens on mobile and test them on real mid-range Android phones.
Avoid horizontal scrolling, tiny text, hover-only interactions and pop-ups that cover the screen.
B4. Thumb-zone navigation and sticky actions
Evidence
Primary actions work best in the lower part of the screen, within natural thumb reach; sticky bottom call-to-action bars keep them visible on long pages [Parachute Design].
Primary navigation should not be hidden behind a hamburger menu unless necessary [Fahrenheit Marketing].
Thumb-zone maps are design heuristics, not guaranteed conversion lifts [Atticus Li].
In practice
Add a sticky bottom bar on mobile with two buttons: "Join a Program" and "Donate".
Keep the top bar simple: logo, language switch and menu. Use a full-screen or bottom-sheet menu with large labelled items.
Keep destructive or secondary actions away from the primary button to avoid mis-taps.
B5. Short, friction-free forms
Evidence
Each extra form field raises abandonment on mobile [Taboola mobile UX guide, 2026].
Non-essential fields can move to a follow-up step after the first submission [Orbit Forms, 2026].
Input text should be at least 16 px to stop iOS auto-zoom, and labels must be attached to fields [mobile UX references].
In practice
Enrolment form: name, mobile number, program and city only. Collect qualification, age and other details after first contact.
Use correct input types (tel, email), autofill attributes and visible labels above fields, never placeholder-only labels.
Show inline, friendly error messages and a clear confirmation screen that states what happens next and when.
Offer WhatsApp as an alternative contact route for people who dislike forms.
B6. A frictionless donation experience
Evidence
A prominent donate button on every page should lead to a fast, mobile-first form with UPI and cards [IRPR NGO guide].
A credible Indian charity site offers UPI, net banking and cards, plus an instant acknowledgement email [Narayan Seva guide].
Donors receive Form 10BE for 80G claims, and 80G needs a traceable, non-cash payment [Smile Foundation].
Girls Who Code's donation page includes a short FAQ explaining how donations are spent [Kanopi].
In practice
Create a dedicated Donate page with preset amounts, a monthly option and "what your gift does" lines (for example, what a month of training costs).
Place UPI QR and deep-link buttons first on mobile, then cards and net banking.
Show 80G, 12A, PAN and Darpan details beside the form and send an instant receipt.
After donating, show a thank-you page with a next step (follow on social, share, volunteer).
B7. Trust and transparency (critical for Indian NGOs)
Evidence
A credible site displays its registration number and makes its 80G certificate downloadable [Narayan Seva guide].
Donors check Darpan ID, 12A and 80G status, audited annual reports and verifiable programmes [Smile Foundation].
No Indian trust, society or Section 8 company may receive foreign donations without FCRA registration or prior permission [Smile Foundation].
Warning signs include donation requests via personal UPI IDs or WhatsApp links only [Smile Foundation].
Visible trust signals are one of seven core items on the 2026 nonprofit website checklist [Elevation Web].
In practice
Add a Transparency page: legal name, registration type and number, PAN, 12A and 80G validity dates, Darpan ID, FCRA status (or a clear note if not applicable), board list, annual report and audited accounts.
Repeat a compact trust strip (registration + 80G badge) in the footer and on the Donate page.
Never route donations through a personal UPI ID; use an institutional account and payment gateway.
Publish an up-to-date Privacy Policy and Terms, and link them from every form.
B8. Imagery and storytelling with dignity
Evidence
People-centred imagery that shows impact is a core best practice [Neon One, 2026].
Photography that shows dignity and agency alongside need builds stronger long-term support than imagery focused only on suffering [KrishaWeb].
In practice
Replace stock photos with consented photographs of real classes, mentors and graduates. Show learners doing, building and presenting, not posing as recipients of charity.
Write stories in the learner's own words: where they started, what they learned, what they do now.
Obtain written consent (and guardian consent for minors) and protect identity where requested. Add descriptive alt text to every image.
B9. Visible impact and social proof
Evidence
The best education nonprofits surface impact early. Code.org shows facts directly below its hero image [Zarla review].
Donors in 2026 are described as more data-driven, so radical transparency and impact evidence matter [Redberries, 2026].
A homepage that talks about the organisation's history instead of its impact is a common mistake [IRPR NGO guide].
In practice
Add an impact strip under the hero with real numbers: learners trained, women supported, batches completed, placements or internships, volunteers.
If numbers are still small, show them honestly with dates; credibility matters more than scale.
Add a testimonials carousel that is keyboard- and swipe-accessible, with a pause control.
Add partner and mentor logos (with permission) once available.
B10. Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 Level AA as the floor
Evidence
WCAG 2.2 AA includes everything from 2.1 AA plus new criteria such as Target Size (Minimum) of 24 by 24 CSS pixels; 44 by 44 is the more comfortable touch size [EZUD; WCAG 2.2 AA reference].
Contrast must be at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text; colour must not be the only way to convey information [MDN mobile accessibility checklist].
Focus indicators need at least 3:1 contrast between focused and unfocused states [TestParty].
Respect the user's reduced-motion setting [example design-system accessibility spec].
In practice
Meet 4.5:1 contrast for all body text and 3:1 for large text and UI borders (see palette in C7).
Make every interactive element reachable by keyboard with a visible focus ring; add a skip-to-content link.
Use semantic headings, landmarks and labelled form controls so screen readers (TalkBack, VoiceOver) work well.
Underline in-text links, avoid text in images, support 200 per cent zoom without loss of content, and honour prefers-reduced-motion.
Provide captions or transcripts for videos and test with real assistive technology, not only automated tools.
B11. Performance and Core Web Vitals
Evidence
Good thresholds are Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) at or under 2.5 s, Interaction to Next Paint (INP) at or under 200 ms, and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) at or under 0.1, at the 75th percentile of real users [corewebvitals.io].
Claims that Google tightened LCP were checked against Google's documentation on 14 August 2026 and the thresholds were unchanged [RGB.ir].
Mobile scores are typically worse than desktop because CPU and network are constrained [Sizzy].
Slow pages lose donors before they load [IRPR NGO guide].
In practice
Compress images to WebP or AVIF, set explicit width and height, and lazy-load below-the-fold media.
Use one or two font families, subset them (include Tamil if used) and preload only the key font.
Keep JavaScript light; avoid heavy sliders, autoplay video and third-party widgets on the homepage.
Test on a throttled 4G connection and a mid-range Android device; monitor field data in Search Console.
B12. Visual identity, typography and layout
Evidence
A cohesive brand identity across site, social media and print builds recognition [Name.com guide, 2026].
Clean, intuitive layouts like charity: water's are repeatedly cited as a benchmark [Big Sea, 2026].
Warm, colourful design with positive imagery gives an inviting feel [CyberOptik].
In practice
Choose a limited palette (primary, accent, neutrals, status colours) with tested contrast, and use it consistently.
Use a clear type scale with generous line height (1.5 for body) and a maximum line length of about 70 characters.
Use a consistent spacing system (8 px grid), card patterns and iconography so pages feel related.
Use the Foundation's logo and one illustration or photo style throughout.
B13. Content, SEO and generative-engine visibility
Evidence
Content that both Google and AI assistants can cite is on the 2026 checklist; preparing for this is called generative engine optimization (GEO) [Elevation Web, 2026].
Homepage copy should be written to answer who, what, why and how [Bloomerang, 2026].
In practice
Write plain-language program pages that answer: who is it for, what will I learn, how long, what does it cost (free), how do I apply.
Add an FAQ and structured data (Organization, NGO, Course, Event) so search engines and AI tools can quote accurate facts.
Use descriptive page titles and meta descriptions, a sitemap and clean URLs.
Publish regular updates (batch completions, events, stories) to show the organisation is active.
B14. Inclusive and bilingual design (recommendation)
This principle is a recommendation based on the Foundation being in Chennai, Tamil Nadu and serving first-generation learners. I did not find research that tests it directly, so treat it as a design judgement to validate with the Foundation's audience.
In practice
Offer English and Tamil with a visible language switch, and translate the key pages first: Home, Programs, Apply, Donate.
Use a well-hinted Tamil web font and test line-breaks, since Tamil text is usually longer and needs larger line height.
Write at a plain reading level, avoid jargon (explain DevOps and cloud in a short phrase), and use icons with text labels.
Support low-end devices and patchy networks: small pages, offline-friendly forms and WhatsApp contact.
B15. Measurement and continuous improvement
Evidence
A website is never finished: wire up analytics and conversion tracking, then fix the biggest leaks first, usually page speed, the donate flow and mobile experience [IRPR NGO guide].
Analytics you actually check is one of seven items on the 2026 nonprofit checklist [Elevation Web].
In practice
Track: enrolment form starts and completions, donation page visits and completions, volunteer sign-ups, scroll depth, and Core Web Vitals.
Review monthly; run one improvement experiment per month (for example, hero headline or button wording).
Collect learner feedback after each batch and use it to update stories and program pages.

C8. Donation flow
Entry points: Donate button in the header, hero, sticky mobile bar and footer, leading to one Donate page.
Choose amount: preset amounts tied to outcomes (for example, the cost of one learner for a month; Foundation to supply real figures), plus "other amount" and a one-time or monthly toggle.
Details: name, email, mobile and PAN (only if the donor wants an 80G receipt), with clear explanation of why PAN is needed.
Pay: UPI (QR on desktop, app deep link on mobile) first, then cards and net banking, through an institutional payment gateway over HTTPS.
Confirm: thank-you page and instant email receipt; Form 10BE and 80G details sent as the tax rules require.
Reassure: beside the form, show registration and 80G details, a short "where your money goes" note, and a contact number for donor queries.
Follow up: invite donors to follow social channels, share, volunteer, or become monthly supporters.

D4. Caveats
Most research sources are agency, vendor or NGO-practice blogs. Treat conversion statistics as directional rather than guaranteed.
Thumb-zone and sticky-bar figures come largely from e-commerce sources; the pattern transfers to nonprofit sites, but the percentages may not.
The Home page was fetched live; the About, Programs, Team and Contact pages were supplied as pasted text. Layout, colours, images and any behaviour that is not visible in text were not reviewed.
The Volunteer page, Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, Sitemap and team bios were not reviewed, so details on those pages are not included.
The Tamil/bilingual recommendation and the suggested palette are design judgements, not research findings. The palette contrast ratios were calculated with the WCAG formula.
Legal and tax points (80G, 12A, FCRA, Form 10BE) are summarised from published guides. The Foundation should confirm its own status and obligations with a qualified adviser before publishing claims.
